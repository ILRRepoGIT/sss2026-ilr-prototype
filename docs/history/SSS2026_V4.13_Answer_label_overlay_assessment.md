# Applying the translator's label overlay (Framework sheet 54) — impact assessment

Industryline Research · internal · 17 September 2026 · companion to the translator handover workbook `SSS2026_Welsh_Translator_Handover_V4.13.xlsx`

## 1. The decision and what this note does

The decision is to give the translator's wording priority over the workbook's, which means applying the 77 rows on Framework sheet 54 (the translator's Welsh for answer labels, filter-group names and the stacked-chart legend where it differs from the live sheets), regenerating the corpus and re-emitting the lock. This note sets out what that changes, where it conflicts with something already ruled, and what the exercise shows about the framework. It is written before applying anything, because the analysis turned up one fact that changes the shape of the decision.

## 2. The fact that changes the decision: the live labels are the survey's own Welsh

The workbook's answer labels (sheet 23) are not our invention. They were copied from sheet 30, the approved bilingual survey instrument — the Welsh that pupils read on screen and chose from. Checking the 77 overlay rows against sheet 30: **at least 53 of the 77 live values are word-for-word the survey's answer options** (more once curly and straight apostrophes are treated alike — the six "Pe bai…" conditions are the survey's too), and the translator's alternatives are therefore *departures from the instrument*, not corrections of an error. Examples:

| Concept | Survey (live in the report) | Translator's document |
|---|---|---|
| Enjoyment scale | Llawer / Ychydig / **Dim Llawer** / Dim o gwbl | … / **Ddim llawer** / Ddim o gwbl |
| Frequency | **1 waith yr wythnos**, 2 waith yr wythnos, Tair gwaith neu fwy yr wythnos | **Unwaith yr wythnos**, Dwywaith yr wythnos, 3 gwaith neu fwy yr wythnos |
| How pupils take part | **Yn sefyll / Yn eistedd** | Sefyll / Eistedd |
| Would do more sport if… | **Pe bai mwy o chwaraeon rydw i'n eu hoffi** (conditional, the survey's stem "Byddwn i'n gwneud mwy o chwaraeon…") | Roedd mwy o chwaraeon rydw i'n eu hoffi (indicative) |
| Don't know / not sure | Dydw i ddim yn gwybod; Dydw i ddim yn siŵr | Ddim yn gwybod; Ddim yn siŵr |
| Prefer not to say | Mae'n well **gen i** beidio â dweud | Mae'n well **gennyf** beidio â dweud |
| Long-term condition | cyflwr **tymor hir** (survey question 6) | cyflwr **hirdymor** |
| PE/Active Lessons scale | Llawer / Eithaf / Dim llawer / Dim o gwbl (one scale for three questions) | Yn iach iawn / Yn hyderus iawn / Yn barod iawn i ddysgu … (a different phrase per question) |

The report quotes these labels inside generated sentences — "dywedodd 92 o'r 366 **'Dim Llawer'** neu **'Dim o gwbl'**" — so the pupils, teachers and governors reading the Welsh report see the words that were in the survey. Replacing them with the translator's wording means the report quotes answers in words the pupils never saw. That is a defensible choice (the translator's forms are arguably more natural Welsh), but it is a choice about fidelity to the instrument, and it should be made knowingly by the owner and the translator, not applied mechanically because "the translator's version takes priority". **The handover workbook therefore puts it to the translator as four explicit questions (sheet 2, AW-1 to AW-4) with our recommendation to keep the survey wording**, and this note recommends that the overlay is applied in one round once those answers are back.

## 3. What applying the overlay changes, row by row

The 77 rows fall into four groups (row counts from sheet 54; corpus counts from the V4.13 payload, 308,400 Welsh records):

**A. Survey answer options quoted in the corpus — 46 rows, ~259,000 record-occurrences (many sentences quote more than one label).** Every "Llawer" (≈21,000 records), "Dim Llawer" (≈8,900), "Dim o gwbl" (≈5,300), "Eithaf" (≈4,200), the six "Pe bai…" conditions (≈11,400), the take-part answers, "Dydw i ddim yn gwybod" (≈500). Applying these changes the generated corpus wholesale: the V4.8 lock (`02c_lock_V48_v7.json`, which has held byte-for-byte through V4.9–V4.13) fails on GOV-lock and GOV-lock-cy and must be re-emitted citing the ruling; sheet 23's per-label gender and initial-class columns must be re-derived for every changed label (a label that heads a cleft — "'Cael hwyl' oedd yr opsiwn a ddewiswyd amlaf" — carries gender for agreement); the jsdom assertions that check legend and label text change; nothing in the English is touched. Decision needed: **AW-1**.

**B. Report-only labels (filter and cohort names, not quoted in sentences) — 13 rows.** "Yr ysgol gyfan → Ysgol Gyfan", "Pob disgybl → Pob Ymatebwr", "Yn gwerthfawrogi: → Gwerthoedd:", "Amcangyfrif o weithiau'n actif… → Amcan o'r amser y mae'n actif…", "Gwrando ar syniadau: → Mae athrawon yn gwrando ar eu syniadau:", "Cymryd rhan: → Yn cymryd rhan:", the ethnicity cohort names. These live on sheet 28/43 (client strings) and the cohort table, appear in the banner, the chips and the "Pupil group" list, and do not enter the narrative (the narrative describes a cohort through sheet 21 audience forms, not through its chip label). **They can be applied without touching the corpus or the lock**, subject to two notes: "Pob Ymatebwr" contradicts the report's own rule of never calling pupils respondents in Welsh (sheet 20; the English "All Respondents" is a locked filter label, EN-05 territory), and the title-case "Ysgol Gyfan" sits beside the lower-case "yr ysgol gyfan" used in every sentence — a casing policy point, not an error.

**C. Rows that conflict with a ruling or the workbook's structure — 6 rows.**
- `ed_combined` Yes/No → "Oes / Nac oes": the ethnically-diverse group is derived from the ethnicity question; no pupil answered yes or no. PR-11 names the set ("Cefndir ethnig amrywiol / Heb fod o gefndir ethnig amrywiol") for exactly this reason. Applying "Oes / Nac oes" invents an answer to a question that was not asked. Decision **AW-3**.
- `pe_feel_*` "Very / Quite / Not very": the translator supplies a different predicate per question (iach / hyderus / parod i ddysgu), and one row even combines all three ("Yn iach/hyderus iawn/yn barod iawn i ddysgu"). Sheet 23 keys a label by its English text, so the three questions share one scale and cannot take twelve distinct labels without a structural change (a metric-qualified key). The survey itself used the shared scale. Decision **AW-2**; if the answer is "question-specific", sheet 23 needs a `metric` column and the payload `optsCy` per metric (the client already reads `optsCy` per metric, so the change is workbook-side and in `build_welsh_lexicon`).
- `ethnicity_group/white` → the long form "Gwyn Cymreig, Saesneg, Albanaidd, …": the survey's top-level option is "Gwyn" and the long form belongs to the follow-up question; the English label in the report is the long form because the English export carries it. Owner question (English side), not a translation question.
- "Other ethnic groups (for example, Arab)" is mapped in the overlay to the generic "Arall" rows of three unrelated questions (take_part_method, most_important, would_do_more_if) — a matching artefact of the V4.10 ingest (same English word "Other"); those three rows must **not** be applied.

**D. Terminology that lives in several sheets — 4 concepts.** "cyflwr tymor hir / hirdymor" appears in sheet 23 (labels), sheet 20/21 (audience components and forms: "a nododd anabledd neu gyflwr tymor hir", ≈10,000 records), sheet 22 (qualifier frames), sheet 28/43 (chart headings, filter labels) and the translator's page text (which uses hirdymor). Likewise "Dydw i ddim yn siŵr / Ddim yn siŵr", "gen i / gennyf", "Yn sefyll / Sefyll". Changing the label alone would leave the sentence forms on the old term — the report would say "'cyflwr hirdymor'" in a chart heading and "a nododd … gyflwr tymor hir" in the sentence under it. Decision **AW-4**, then a coordinated multi-sheet edit.

## 4. What this shows about the framework

1. **There is no single terminology table.** A concept's Welsh is held separately in sheets 20, 21, 22, 23, 28, 43 and 53, with nothing tying them together. The "tymor hir / hirdymor" split is the symptom: the translator changed the page text, the labels still carry the survey form, and the engine's audience forms carry a third copy. A `Terminology` sheet (concept → one Welsh form, with the sheets that consume it) referenced by the other sheets, plus a gate that fails when two sheets disagree, would have caught this at v2.3. This is the one structural change worth making before production.
2. **Answer labels are keyed by English text, not by question + code.** That is why three PE/Active-Lessons questions share one scale and why "Other" matched across unrelated questions. A metric-qualified key (`metric/code`) is a small change in sheet 23 and `build_welsh_lexicon`; the client already consumes labels per metric.
3. **Sheet 23 duplicates sheet 30 without asserting the link.** Today they agree (that is how we know the live labels are the survey's), but nothing enforces it, and a well-meant "correction" on sheet 23 would silently break fidelity to the instrument. A `LBL-survey` gate (every sheet-23 label equals its sheet-30 option, or carries a signed exception) would make the AW-1 decision explicit and permanent.
4. **The casing policy covers sport names only (D21).** The survey's "Dim Llawer" (capital L) is reproduced as the pupils saw it; the translator read it as an error. Whichever way AW-1 goes, the policy should say whether quoted labels keep the instrument's casing.
5. **"Dim" versus "Ddim" is a real grammatical distinction the framework does not model.** "Dim llawer" is the standalone list form; "ddim llawer" is the mutated predicative form inside a sentence ("dydw i ddim yn…"). The engine quotes labels verbatim inside sentences, which is safe (quotation blocks mutation) but is why the translator's "Ddim" forms look wrong in a list and the survey's "Dim" forms look wrong to a translator thinking of running prose. The rule "quoted labels are never mutated" (sheet 4, rule 36 in the handover) is the right rule; it should be stated in the framework (sheet 07 notes) rather than being implicit in the realiser.
6. **Figures in labels.** "1 waith yr wythnos" (survey) versus "Unwaith yr wythnos" (translator) is the digit policy (D02) meeting the instrument. D02 governs generated statistics, not quoted labels; the workbook should say which wins for labels.

None of these is a weakness in the mutation, numeral, article or relative-clause rules themselves; the handover's sheet 4 (40 rules with real sentences) and sheet 5 (40 sentences) are where those get their first native-speaker check. One suspected engine defect was found while assembling those sheets and is flagged there for the translator: "Yr ail camp neu weithgaredd mwyaf cyffredin" — *camp* should soft-mutate after *ail* ("Yr ail gamp") — 2,465 records in this school's corpus, so a one-line realiser fix once confirmed; and the "ac 36" boundary (CONJ-06, vigesimal reading) is flagged for the translator to confirm which reading Welsh readers actually use.

## 5. Recommended sequence

1. Send the handover workbook; wait for AW-1–AW-4 and the sheet-2 term decisions (disability_condition, ui144/settings_ed, ui.stack_caption_sex).
2. Framework v2.5: add the Terminology sheet; key sheet 23 by metric/code; apply group B now, group A/C/D exactly as answered; record every applied row on the v2.5 change log with "translator, document 15 Sep 2026, confirmed <date>".
3. Rebuild (V4.14): expect GOV-lock to fail against the V4.8 lock if any group-A row is applied; re-emit `02c_lock_V414_v7.json` citing the change-log rows; re-baseline the jsdom label assertions; corpus comparison report listing the changed records by label.
4. Add the `LBL-survey` gate and the terminology-consistency gate to the vendored pack request (pack owners, alongside STK-caption-case).

Effort: about half a day of workbook and lexicon work plus one full build, once the answers are in. Applying the overlay before the answers would mean doing it twice.
