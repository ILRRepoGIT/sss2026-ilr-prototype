Introduction

Welcome to your school’s results

The School Sport Survey is one of the largest per-capita opportunities in the world for children and young people to have their say on sport. The survey would not be possible without the time and commitment of schools, teachers and pupils like you. Thank you for helping us build a clearer picture of how children and young people experience sport in Wales, and how they want sport to look in the future.

Your school’s findings are presented in line with the Vision for Sport in Wales across four chapters:

  • An Active Nation for Everyone
  • A Lifelong Enjoyment of Sport
  • Sport Specific Data
  • Appendix

Within each section, you’ll find questions that aim to help consider the findings and what they might mean for your school. We hope it helps you celebrate what’s working well, identify opportunities for development, and support conversations about how to create even more positive sporting experiences for all learners.

Together, your responses are helping Sport Wales, schools and partners across Wales better understand what matters most to children and young people, ensuring that future decisions are informed by their voices.

    What is an interactive report?

    Unlike a static report, this report can be explored in more detail by applying filters. When one or more filters are applied, the charts and written findings update together to present a new view of the data captured in your school, based on the filters you have selected. In turn, you can view, save and print multiple versions of the report through many different lenses. You can learn how to apply filters by reading the ‘How do I use filters?’ tab below.

    How do I use filters?

    There are two ways to use filters in this report:

    • Use the Explore results panel on the right-hand side of your screen to select specific groups of pupils.
    • You can also click on bar charts to filter the report by the pupils who selected that response option.

    By clicking on the different filters, the whole report will adjust to show information for that specific group. You can then print or save the report with those filters applied. You can reset the report by clicking “Reset to whole school”. This will clear all filters.

    What does the “You are viewing” banner at the top of the page tell me?

    It always shows which pupils you are looking at and how many pupil responses are included — so every number on screen can be read in context. For example:

    What do the different colours mean?

    Bars alternate between red and blue to help distinguish different answer categories. These colours do not mean that one answer is better or worse than another. Light grey marks bars that cannot be selected as filters, and dark grey marks suppressed values. When a value is selected to explore that group’s other responses, the value turns yellow and is marked as selected — matching the selected-group box in the Explore results panel.

    Why are some bars grey, blank or not clickable?

    A light grey bar cannot be selected as a filter. This includes response options that did not meet the whole-school eligibility threshold of five for use as a filter. Counts below five may still be displayed within an otherwise reportable demographic view. A dark grey bar marked “—” is suppressed: the value is not reportable in the current view.

    What is the ‘base’ number?

    Each chart says “Based on: X pupils”. This is the total number of pupils who answered that particular question. Some questions were asked only of certain pupils, so the ‘base’ number can differ from chart to chart.

    What do the numbers tell me?

    Results are unweighted counts of pupil responses — real answers from real pupils, with nothing scaled up. Every sentence names the group it describes. A worked example: “Boxing and football were tied as the most frequently selected sports, with seven selections each, among Year 5 boys” — “tied” means both were selected by the same number of pupils, and “among Year 5 boys” tells you the finding applies only to that group.

    What do the percentages tell me?

    Percentages represent the share of the base. In other words, a percentage shows the proportion of pupils who chose that response option, out of the total number of pupils who answered that question. Percentages appear only in whole-school and phase views, always together with their count and base, because percentages of very small groups can mislead.

    Why don’t some percentages add to 100%?

    There are two reasons why percentages may not add to 100%. Firstly, figures are rounded to the nearest whole percent, and so overall figures may not add exactly to 100%. Secondly, where pupils could tick more than one answer, the percentages describe the proportion of pupils who selected that option, rather than segments of a whole.

    Why don’t some numbers add to the total number of pupil responses?

    Some questions were only asked of certain pupils (for example, follow-up questions about a setting are asked only of pupils who used that setting), some pupils left questions blank, and multi-choice questions count every selection — so totals can be smaller or larger than the number of pupil responses. Use the Base number to help understand who those results are representing.

    Why do some numbers become hidden after applying filters?

    Responses become hidden when there is a risk of being able to identify an individual pupil via the information presented. In turn, information is suppressed to protect pupil identity, in line with GDPR. This often happens when too many filters are applied, and pupil numbers fall low enough to potentially identify the pupil who gave those responses.

    What do the grey bars and greyed-out sections mean?

    Dark grey bars represent suppressed data — values that cannot be shown because the group behind them is too small to report safely. Light grey bars are options that cannot be selected as filters, as the data can become misleading when not considered in the context of the way the question was asked. Whole sections may also appear greyed out with a message when the group you have selected is too small to provide insights. If you continue scrolling, there may still be insights and information in other sections that have not been suppressed, depending on the amount of data available.

    What are Free School Meal Quartiles?

    Sport Wales uses the proportion of pupils eligible for Free School Meals (FSM) as an indicator of deprivation. Schools are grouped into four FSM quartiles based on the proportion of pupils eligible for Free School Meals.

    FSM Q1 includes schools with the lowest proportion of pupils eligible for FSM. This quartile represents the 25% of schools with the lowest levels of deprivation.

    FSM Q2 includes schools with a lower-than-average proportion of pupils eligible for FSM, but not the lowest. This quartile represents the 25% of schools with below-average levels of deprivation, but not the lowest levels.

    FSM Q3 includes schools with a higher-than-average proportion of pupils eligible for FSM, but not the highest. This quartile represents the 25% of schools with above-average levels of deprivation, but not the highest levels.

    FSM Q4 includes schools with the highest proportion of pupils eligible for FSM. This quartile represents the 25% of schools with the highest levels of deprivation in Wales.

    Note: These quartiles provide a high-level measure of deprivation at school level. They do not indicate the individual circumstances of pupils attending those schools. Pupils experiencing deprivation may attend schools in any FSM quartile.

    How are our pupils active through sport in School?

    We captured pupils’ sporting habits across two school-based contexts: during lesson time (i.e., curricular) and at school sport clubs (i.e., extracurricular).     Unmet demand

    Unmet demand refers to sports that pupils said they would like to do more of, but have not participated in since the start of the academic year. This may highlight where pupils have demand that is not currently being met. Please note, the 2026 survey newly captures information on sports pupils did in PE or Lesson time. In turn, Unmet Demand is calculated differently in 2026 compared with 2022, as it accounts for sports done in PE or Lesson Time, whilst previous Unmet Demand calculations could not. Caution is therefore advised if comparing with previous survey findings.
